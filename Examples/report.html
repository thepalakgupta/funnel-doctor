The funnel

Out of every 100 real visitors who view a product, about 24 add to cart and about 7 buy.

Clean traffic = all users except the 4,347 ghost carts. No buyers are removed. Counts are from the 10% sample (×10 ≈ whole store).

Finding 14,347 "ghost carts" fake a checkout problem

On Oct 2, 6 and 7, thousands of new users each viewed one product, hit "add to cart" 1–3 times within seconds, and vanished. 31% of them did it between midnight and 6 am UTC (real carters: 9%). That doesn't look human.

They are 43% of everyone who carted and didn't buy. Strip them out and cart → buy jumps from 19.0% to 29.0%. Good news hidden underneath: the real users from those days bought at 9.6% overall, better than the regular 6.5%.

Do: find the source (referrer, UTM, IP, user agent) and filter it out of every KPI and test.

Finding 2Small baskets almost never check out

Carts worth $10 or less convert at just 6.0%. Above $100, it's 54.1%. Single-item carts: 7.6% vs 39.1% for 2+ items.

Carts of $20 or less are 58% of real carted-not-bought users (3,372 of 5,854). Likely cause: a shipping fee or minimum order that makes a $3 order not worth finishing. That's a hypothesis to confirm with ops.

Do: test an "Add $X more for free shipping" progress bar in the cart.

Finding 3Carters who leave on day one rarely come back

19.5% vs 41%

Buy rate of carters active on only one day vs those who came back. That's 3,676 one-day non-buyers, 63% of all real non-buyers. Yet 29% of buyers buy more than 24 hours after their first cart, so bringing people back works.

Caveat: part of this is reverse causality. People who mean to buy come back in order to buy.

Do: test abandoned-cart reminders at 1 h and 24 h, once you know how many carters you can email.

Methodology, definitions & caveats
  • Data: every user whose ID ends in 0 (~10% of users, full journeys), 407,521 events. 5% exact-duplicate rows removed. Times are UTC.
  • Funnel (unique users, nested): Viewed = any view · Carted = viewed and any add-to-cart · Bought = carted and any purchase in October.
  • Ghost cart: first seen Oct 2, 6 or 7, and the whole month is 1 view + 1–3 cart events of one product. "Clean" = everyone else.
  • Cart value = sum of prices of distinct products carted in the month. A purchase row is a line item, not an order.
  • Caveats: no device, referrer, shipping or campaign data, so every "why" is a hypothesis. Patterns are correlations, not proven causes.
